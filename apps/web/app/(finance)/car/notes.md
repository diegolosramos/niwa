# How to buy a car

# New or used

You should only buy a new car if you're a millionaire. The depreciation cost is too high for most people, making used cars the only financially sensible option.

# The true cost of owning a car

- Depreciation: The loss in value of the car over time.
- Insurance: The cost of insuring the car.
- Maintenance: The cost of regular maintenance and repairs.
- Fuel: The cost of fuel for the car.
- Financing: The cost of any loans or interest payments for the car.
- Taxes and registration: The cost of registering the car and paying any applicable taxes.

# Recommendations

Your monthly car expenses should not exceed 10% of your gross income.

The car price should not exceed 35% of your gross income and should be closer to 20% for optimal financial health.

Used cars with warranty are always a safer option.

# Fuel cost

How to estimate fuel costs for your car. Consider the following factors:

- Fuel efficiency: The average miles per gallon (MPG) your car achieves.
- Distance driven: The total miles you drive per year.
- Average fuel price: The current cost of fuel per gallon.

Fuel cost can be estimated using the formula:

```
Fuel Cost = (Distance Driven / Fuel Efficiency) * Fuel Price
```

# Financing 

It's often cheaper to finance through a bank. Get a pre-approval for a car loan before visiting the dealership.

When financing a car, consider the following factors:

- Interest rate: The annual interest rate on the car loan.
- Loan term: The duration of the car loan, typically expressed in months or years.
- Down payment: The initial amount paid upfront when purchasing the car.
- Monthly payment: The amount to be paid each month towards the car loan.
- Total cost: The overall cost of the car including the down payment, interest, and any fees associated with the loan.

# Shop for insurance

Once you're close to purchasing a car, it's important to shop for insurance to ensure you have the necessary coverage in place before driving the vehicle off the lot.

Get multiple quotes to get a better price.

# Integrity checklist for a car

Any item with ** indicated a deal-breaker or a significant issue that requires careful consideration.

Step 0: Ask about the car

1. Why are you selling this car? (only for private seller)
2. Is there any issue with this car that I should be aware of?

Pay close attention to the sellers body language and any hesitation in their responses, as it may indicate potential issues with the car.

Step 1: Research the car's history and condition.

Use kbb.com to check the car's market value and history.
Additionally, obtain a vehicle history report from services like Carfax or AutoCheck to verify the car's past ownership, accidents, and maintenance records.

Also, research the car model's reliability, common issues, engine issues, and owner reviews to get a better understanding of what to expect.

Check at what point warranty expires and what it covers.

- [] Car's price range
- [] Vehicle history report obtained
- [] Reliability and common issues researched
- [] Warranty checked
- [] Engine issues researched

Step 2: Verify the car's title and ownership.

- [] Title and ownership verified**

Step 3: Inspect the car thoroughly

### Engine inspection**

- [] Check the engine to see if it's warm
- [] The engine is clean
- [] The battery terminals are clean and corrosion-free
- [] The belts and hoses are in good condition
- [] Check oil level and condition

At this stage, place a cardboard under the engine to check for any fluid leaks.

- [] The car doesn't struggle to start
- [] No fluid leaks detected under the engine (wait 10 minutes with the engine running) **

For the cardboard check, looks for fluid leaks or stains that are not a/c condensation.

### Exterior inspection

Walk around the car, touch all defects with your finger and have the seller notice you see the problems.

- [] Checked the exterior for any dents, scratches, or rust
- [] Checked the tires for tread wear and proper inflation
- [] Checked the windows for any cracks or damage
- [] Checked the mirrors for any cracks or damage
- [] Checked the paint for any inconsistencies or damage
- [] Checked the tires for tread wear and proper inflation
- [] All doors open and close properly
- [] Gas tank lid opens and closes properly
- [] Trunk opens and closes properly

### Interior inspection

Take your time. Slow down.

- [] There are no issue indicators in the odometer.
- [] The seats are in good condition. Not torn or stained.
- [] The dashboard is in good condition
- [] The A/C is functioning properly
- [] The touchscreen console is functioning properly
- [] All buttons are functioning properly
- [] The headlights, taillights and turn signals are functioning properly
- [] The floor mats and carpeting are in good condition
- [] All windows and the sunroof (if applicable) opens and closes properly
- [] The bluetooth is functioning properly
- [] The USB ports are functioning properly
- [] The speakers are functioning properly
- [] The horn is functioning properly
- [] The seatbelts are functioning properly
- [] The emergency brake is functioning properly
- [] The windshield wipers are functioning properly


### Test drive

Drive the car for 30 to 60 minutes, make sure to test on the highway at high speeds. While test driving, do not speak and turn off the radio to better hear any unusual noises.

- [] The car accelerates smoothly and without unusual noises
- [] The steering wheel responds correctly and without unusual noises
- [] The steering wheel does not pull to one side and remains stable during driving
- [] The steering wheel does not vibrate during driving
- [] The brakes respond correctly and without unusual noises
- [] The brakes do not pull to one side during braking
- [] The pedals are sturdy and respond correctly during driving

### For four-wheel drive cars

- [] Test the four-wheel drive system to ensure it engages and disengages properly
- [] Check for any unusual noises or vibrations when the four-wheel drive is engaged
- [] Vehicle can get over a moderate obstacle like a curb without scraping the undercarriage

### Negotiation

For dealerships, prefer to negotiate online.

List any and all issues or concerns you have identified during the inspection and test drive to use as leverage during negotiation.

Ask for a price after doing so. Do not make an offer first. Don't say a number.

### Tips

For cash transactions, make sure to do them on a safe, public place like a bank parking lot.
Always count the money in front of the seller and get a receipt for the transaction.
Consider bringing a friend or family member for added security during the transaction.

Buying on a dealership is safer and easier on the paperwork and legal aspects, but more expensive compared to private sellers.

If you have enough money to pay cash and you're buying on a dealership, you're likely better off financing the car through the dealership to take advantage of any promotional interest rates or incentives, rather than paying the full amount upfront. Then pay the car early. Make sure there are no early payment penalties.

Dealers want you to finance the car to make more money from you.
