# How I want to invest

Based on my research the best approach for me is to create a 3 Fund portfolio, comprised of $VOO, $VEA, and $BND. I will simulate the behavior of a Target Retirement Fund (TRF), manually, changing the allocation over time as I age.

I decided to avoid TRFs because I want more control over how aggressive I invest my money and when I adjust the allocation to bonds. I think TRFs are too conservative early in life.

## $VOO 

$VOO is an ETF that tracks the S&P 500 index, representing large-cap U.S. stocks. It provides broad exposure to the U.S. equity market and is a core component of a diversified portfolio. Historically, it has offered strong long-term growth potential, though it comes with market volatility risk.

## $VEA

$VEA is an ETF that tracks the MSCI EAFE index, representing large- and mid-cap stocks in developed markets outside the U.S. and Canada. It provides international diversification and can help reduce the overall risk of a portfolio by spreading investments across multiple countries and regions.

## $BND

$BND is an ETF that tracks the Bloomberg Barclays U.S. Aggregate Bond Index, representing a broad range of U.S. investment-grade bonds. It provides income and stability to a portfolio, helping to balance the higher volatility of equities and reduce overall portfolio risk.

## Notes

This strategy is effective because it eliminates the need to think about allocation decisions. The decisions will be made once a year at most. Once selected, the focus can shift towards increasing the savings rate. Savings Rate is FAR MORE important than the rate of returns.

Different investment strategies can be explored after a high enough net worth is achieved, like $500,000. At that point, one might consider more aggressive growth strategies, alternative investments, or tax optimization techniques to further enhance portfolio performance.

### Bitcoin

Bitcoin is not a good investment because it's too volatile, speculative and it doesn't provide a service or generate income like traditional investments such as stocks or bonds.

Also, there are plenty of issues buying Bitcoin

- ETF expense ratios are 0.14% at a min with low liquidity compared to traditional stock and bond ETFs.
- There are regulatory concerns and taxes can be complicated if self-custodied.
- Self-custody eliminates expense ratio, but introduces the risk of losing access to the Bitcoin if the private keys are lost or stolen.
- Real Bitcoin transactions are slow and expensive
